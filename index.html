04 · The content philosophy

When and why.
Not how-to.

Anyone can explain a routine. Not everyone can tell you when it works, when it doesn't, and why. That judgment is what William sells, so that is what we put on camera.

We don't make

Step-by-step tutorials. Generic routines and splits. Information anyone can find in five minutes.

We make

Judgment. When something applies and when it doesn't. The reasoning behind a stance. Lessons earned the hard way.

Every video is A to B

Each ~10 minute video attacks one pain point of William's ICP, men 18-30 who want to rebuild their body and their mind. A is that pain point as the viewer lives it. B is where they need to be. They leave with a clear shift in how they think, not a list of exercises. The pain points come from the ICP's real problems and client testimonials.

Every script runs on four beats

The claim

A stance William actually holds, often against the common advice.

The when

The situation where it applies, and where it doesn't.

The why

The mechanism behind it.

The proof

His own story, a client pattern, or what it cost him to learn.

Each beat is a planned clip. Together they make the video.

Next steps

Two things before the first script

Questions for William to answer after the call. They are the raw material for everything above, so answer with what you actually believe, not what sounds good.

01

Your contrarian takes

What it is. A contrarian take flips a common belief in your space because you genuinely see it differently. It comes from your own experience, and it is the claim a script is built on. It is not the same as a controversial take, which is engineered to trigger people. Only write down beliefs you really hold.

Reference example, not your answer

Common belief: "Wait until you feel motivated, then start."

Contrarian take: "Motivation is the wrong tool. Build a standard that holds on your worst day, because that's the only day that counts."

Your questions
  1. What are 4 things people in fitness and self-improvement say or do that you disagree with?
  2. For each one, what is the opposite, the approach you would teach instead?
  3. For each one, why do you disagree? What did you live, see in a client or learn the hard way?
  4. Go back through the list. Do you truly believe each one, or does it just sound good? Cross out anything that is only there to get a reaction.
  5. Which one or two would you be happy to repeat every week for a year? That is your core contrarian take.
02

Your associations

What it is. A brand is what people connect your name with. If you don't choose those connections, your audience will choose them for you. So you decide two things: what you are known for, and what you are known as being against. Together they become the filter for every video, clip and partnership.

Reference example, not your answer
I'm for

Discipline that holds on bad days, not motivation.
Training the body and the mind as one system.

I'm against

Shortcuts sold as transformation.
Coaches who tell you what you want to hear.

Your questions
  1. Write 2 things you want people to instantly connect you with. These are your "for" associations.
  2. Write 2 things you want people to know you are firmly against. These are your "against" associations.
  3. For each of the four, write one sentence on why it matters to you.
  4. Check all four. Would you still stand behind each one in a year, with a client watching? If not, replace it.
